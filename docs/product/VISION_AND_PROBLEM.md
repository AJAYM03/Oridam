# Vision and Problem

## The False Problem
"Users need more free storage."

This is incorrect. Users already know how to get infinite free storage: they just create another Google account. Google solved storage. Users solved capacity.

## The Real Problem
**Fragmentation and Cognitive Load.**

The trigger is almost always the same: **"Google Photos says my storage is full."**
When a user creates their second, third, or fourth Google account to solve this, they trade storage capacity for management overhead. Every file interaction becomes a decision:
- *Where is this file?*
- *Which account has enough space to receive this upload?*
- *Did I put my tax documents in my personal account or my backup account?*

Nobody solved fragmentation.

## The Personas

### Persona A: The Organizer
They already have 5 accounts. They have the storage they need. 
**Their pain:** Finding things and constantly context switching between tabs and logins.

### Persona B: The Avoider
They know they *could* create another account to avoid paying for storage, but they don't.
**Their pain:** The mental overhead of managing a fragmented system is worse than the problem of running out of space.

## The Vision
**Use all your Google accounts as one.**

Oridam solves both personas by abstracting the infrastructure entirely. 
The user's mental model is "I have multiple Google accounts." Oridam unifies the storage across those accounts into one seamless experience.

For **The Organizer**: Keep using your multiple accounts. We'll make them feel like one.
For **The Avoider**: Go ahead and create that second account. You won't ever have to manage it.

## The Core Product Tenet
Oridam is not a tool to bypass Google's 15GB limit. 
The innovation is removing the management overhead that comes *after* creating multiple accounts.

The product succeeds when a user never has to ask, *"Which account is this in?"*
